# Transient Labs Stacks Protocol
The Stacks Protocol consists of sales contracts for the Transient Labs ecosystems. Sales comprise of mints, auctions, and buy nows. More can be added in the future as well.

## Mints
Two contracts for minting new NFTs to ERC-721 and ERC-1155 contracts. They are designed for Transient Labs creator contracts; other integrations must implement the expected `externalMint` signature (different for 721 vs 1155) and owner/admin/mint-approval interfaces. ERC-1155 integrations must also implement `getTokenDetails(tokenId)` with the `IERC1155TL.Token` return format.

The mint contracts are
- `TLStacks721.sol`
- `TLStacks1155.sol`

### Features
- multi-tenant sales which keeps NFT contracts lighter weight with less code
- implements a flat buyers fee in ETH per NFT minted on ETH drops. ERC-20 drops charge no ETH, so a purchase only needs the drop currency (friendly to cross-chain fillers).
- seller fee as a percentage of the sale, with separate rates for ETH and ERC-20 drops, each capped at 5% (`MAX_SELLER_FEE_BPS`)
- the contract owner can waive the seller fee for a drop by supplying its `expectedRound`. Stale waivers revert with `DropRoundChanged`; a deliberate waiver of the current empty round remains supported. Applied waivers reset when a new round starts.
- configurable for any allowlisted ERC-20 token (the contract owner manages the allowlist; native currency is always allowed)
- drop admins can decrease a live drop's total supply (never below the number already minted, and never above the configured supply)
- velocity/marathon mint: negative `decayRate` shortens the duration and positive extends it, in seconds per minted token. A batch is admitted atomically based on the phase when its transaction executes, then applies the duration change for the entire batch; it is not rechecked as sequential single-token purchases. Negative reductions saturate at zero before multiplication; `int256.min` is rejected at configuration and rate updates with `InvalidDecayRate`
- drop end timestamps (`startTime + presaleDuration + publicDuration`) must fit in `uint64`. Configuration, schedule updates, and marathon mints that would exceed this limit revert with `InvalidDropDuration`; stored fields and function parameters remain `uint256`
- selectable mint (ERC-721 only): buyers pick which 0-based pieces of the drop they mint, and each piece can only be minted once per drop round
- only the NFT contract owner or admins can configure/alter/cancel a mint
- ERC-1155 tokens must be created before configuring their drops (`getTokenDetails(tokenId).created` must be true). Created tokens with zero minted supply are supported; future token IDs cannot be preconfigured
- purchases specify the expected currency, max cost per token (excluding the protocol fee), and drop round. They reject a currency or round mismatch or a token price above the submitted maximum. Phase selection and allowance accounting use the drop state at execution time; purchases are not bound to the phase observed when submitted

Presale integrations must use the [exact Merkle leaf and proof format](docs/presale-merkle.md), including the recipient pre-hash and double-hashed leaf. Fixed vectors cover both minters and selectable purchases.

### Trust Assumptions & Known Limitations
- **Seller fees on active drops:** both minters use the current global ETH or ERC-20 seller-fee rate when a purchase executes, within the 5% cap and subject to the drop round's fee waiver. The protocol owner can change that rate during an active drop, changing seller proceeds without changing the buyer's token price or drop round. Collection admins may close drops or revoke minting access, but have no guaranteed response window before a fee change applies. Seller fees are not snapshotted and no collection-specific fee ceiling is enforced.
- **Seller-fee rounding:** the seller fee is computed on each purchase's total (`numberToMint * cost`) and rounds down in favor of the seller. Splitting a purchase into smaller transactions can therefore lower the total fee collected, by less than one base unit of the currency per purchase. It is most visible when `cost * feeBps` is below `10_000` base units (very low prices or low-decimal ERC-20s), where a single-token purchase pays no seller fee at all. This is accepted as negligible.
- **ERC-20 mint payout compatibility:** direct payments verify that the recipient's balance increases by at least the full payment amount when `transferFrom` returns. This safety check rejects short payments. Token/receiver combinations that immediately forward funds during a transfer callback can fail the check and are unsupported; the purchase reverts atomically. Allowlisted currencies and seller/fee receivers must be compatible with this balance check. Payments are not routed through temporary custody to accommodate forwarding callbacks.
- **Atomic mint payouts:** both minters forward available gas for ETH payouts. Payment and minting must succeed together: a receiver that rejects ETH or consumes too much gas can revert the purchase, rolling back payments and mint state while costing the buyer transaction gas. Collection admins and the protocol owner are responsible for selecting usable seller and fee receivers; integrations should simulate purchases to detect current failures. Minters do not create unpaid-proceeds credits or apply the auction house's configurable payout gas allowance.
- **Mint protocol-fee receiver:** the protocol owner is responsible for ensuring this receiver is not sanctioned, monitoring its status, and rotating it when necessary. Both mint contracts screen it when fee settings are configured, but intentionally omit purchase-time rechecks to avoid the additional gas. Fees can continue reaching a receiver whose oracle status changes after configuration until the owner acts. Purchase-time screening of the buyer, mint recipient, and seller payout receiver remains in place when an oracle is configured; `address(0)` disables screening.
- **Collection administration and coordination:** `TLStacks721` and `TLStacks1155` trust the NFT collection owner and authorized collection admins to manage the current drop. Administrative calls apply to the drop state at execution time and are not bound to the round observed when the transaction was prepared; the caller must still be authorized when it executes. Teams using multiple admin wallets or independently executable smart-account operations are responsible for coordinating changes and sequencing or invalidating pending operations. A delayed update or closure can affect a replacement drop, and a delayed configuration can restore older terms when configuration is permitted. Ordinary sequential transactions from one EOA preserve that wallet's nonce order. This trust assumption concerns collection administration, not protocol-owner fee waivers or buyer-supplied purchase constraints.
- **Delegated minting and gifting:** the payer and recipient may differ, but presale and public mint allowances are charged to the recipient. A payer who can satisfy the purchase requirements can therefore consume a recipient's phase allowance by minting tokens to them. This is intentional so gifts and delegated presale mints do not require recipient authorization. The caller pays the full mint price and fees, and the recipient receives the tokens.
- **Purchase phase at execution:** a pending presale purchase can execute during the public sale if the round, currency, price limit, remaining supply, recipient allowance, and other purchase checks still pass. It then consumes the recipient's public-sale allowance rather than presale allowance; presale eligibility does not reserve that phase or allocation. ETH purchases must also supply exactly the live token cost plus the applicable buyer protocol fee. This applies to natural phase transitions and administrator changes to the schedule. Integrations should disclose this behavior; no buyer-supplied expected phase is enforced.
- **Selectable collection size:** selectable minting is intended for relatively small collections. A 10,000-piece collection is comfortably within the measured range for `getMintedIndices`; this is guidance, not an enforced supply cap or an RPC guarantee. The getter reads one bitmap word per 256 configured pieces, including unminted pieces, so very large supplies can exceed RPC gas or response limits. `isIndexMinted` checks an individual index without reading the full bitmap. Pagination is not provided.
- **Selectable mint compatibility:** selectable drops require an NFT implementation that honors the per-selection URI supplied to `externalMint`. `ERC7160TLEditions` is not compatible with this use case: it ignores that URI and lets holders choose among shared collection metadata. The Stacks bitmap tracks purchased indices; it cannot enforce the selected metadata or unique displayed artwork on Editions. Integrations should not offer selectable drops for Editions. This restriction is documented but is not enforced by an on-chain implementation check.
- **Selectable mint ordering:** selected indices are unique and visible in pending transaction calldata. Another eligible buyer who observes the transaction can mint an index first, causing the pending purchase to revert. Appropriate pricing and private transaction routing can reduce the practical incentive and exposure, but private routing is not guaranteed across wallets and networks. A conflict late in a batch can waste gas spent processing earlier indices; payments, supply, allowances, and bitmap changes still roll back atomically. Batch-gas optimization is deferred.

## Auction House
A contract for auctions and buy nows for minted ERC-721 tokens. Supported collections must permit the escrow transfers described below.

The auction house contract is `TLAuctionHouse.sol`

### Features
- protocol fee as a percentage of the sale, charged to the seller
- the initial configured auction duration is limited to 30 days. Accepted regular bids in the last five minutes restore five minutes of remaining time as anti-snipe protection, including self-bids meeting the normal minimum increase and funding requirements. These extensions have no cumulative duration cap, so the total auction duration can exceed 30 days
- listings can be scheduled ahead of time
- configurable for any allowlisted ERC-20 token (the contract owner manages the allowlist; native currency is always allowed)
- respects royalties on secondary sales (if configured on the token)
- royalty recipients and amounts are determined before the NFT leaves escrow, but the NFT is delivered before any payout; an accepted engine schedule may include the auction house alongside another payable recipient, in which case the auction-house share is skipped and left with the seller's chosen payout receiver
- listing types
    - Scheduled auction
    - Reserve auction
    - Reserve auction + buy now
    - Buy now
    - Private sale (fixed price, and the NFT is always delivered to the recipient set by the seller, though anyone can pay)
- multiple NFTs can be listed in one transaction via `batchList` (if any listing fails, the whole batch reverts)
- only the NFT owner can configure or cancel an listing
- sellers can update a listing via `updateListing` instead of canceling and relisting, which changes everything except the listing type and currency. Updates aren't allowed once an auction has been bid on, and each update gets a new listing id so pending bids and buys signed against the old terms revert
- sellers can change the payout receiver at any time before settlement via `updatePayoutReceiver`, which keeps the listing id since bidders' terms don't change
- the highest bidder can add any positive amount to their bid via `topUpBid` while more than five minutes remain; top ups never extend the auction. At five minutes or less remaining, top ups revert with `TopUpWindowClosed`; use a regular bid, meeting the 5% minimum increase, which restores the normal five-minute response window when needed
- the NFT is always delivered to the buyer before funds are released to the seller; settlement either fully completes or reverts, and anyone can settle an ended auction
- if an auction can't be settled (e.g. a broken NFT contract or a sanctioned party), the highest bidder can cancel it via `cancelUnsettledAuction` 7 days after the auction ends and the seller can cancel it 30 days after the auction ends. Cancellation credits the bid to the bidder (`claimableFunds`) and holds the NFT for the seller (`claimableNft`)
- failed payments become credits when the operation can complete. The beneficiary can redirect their own credit with `claimFunds`; anyone can call `retryPayout(beneficiary, currencyAddress)` to deliver only to that beneficiary. Both are available while paused, require screening, and preserve credits on transfer failure. Permissionless retry adds no admin redirection and cannot rescue a permanently incompatible destination
- NFTs from canceled unsettled auctions are held for the seller and can be claimed via `claimNft`, subject to the NFT contract permitting the transfer; anything owed to a sanctioned address stays frozen until the address is no longer sanctioned
- bids specify the expected listing id and currency, and buy nows specify the expected currency and max price, to guard against front-running (e.g. a seller delisting and relisting with a longer auction duration while a bid is pending)
- the minimum next bid is the current bid plus 5%, rounded up

### Trust Assumptions & Known Limitations
- **Contract bidders:** the winning bidder receives the NFT through `transferFrom`, without an `onERC721Received` acceptance callback. Before bidding, contract wallets and integrations must confirm they can manage or transfer that NFT after delivery. A contract without suitable execution or transfer capabilities can strand its award; the auction house provides no admin recovery after successful delivery. A missing receiver callback alone does not imply the contract lacks those capabilities.
- **NFT transfer compatibility:** the auction house uses `transferFrom` for escrow deposits and delivery, checks ownership after each transfer, and does not implement `onERC721Received`. Collections that require this receiver callback even during `transferFrom` are unsupported: listing reverts atomically and the NFT remains with its owner. Integrations should verify both deposit and delivery behavior for supported collections.
- **Direct ERC-20 deposits:** do not transfer tokens directly to the auction-house address. Use `bid`, `topUpBid`, or `buyNow` so the contract can account for the payment. A raw token transfer creates no beneficiary credit and cannot be recovered through `claimFunds` or `retryPayout`. There is no admin sweep or recovery function for mistaken deposits.
- **ERC-20 allowlist:** currencies must be chosen carefully by the protocol owner; ERC-20 interface compatibility alone is not sufficient. Before allowlisting, review actual transfer behavior, issuer freeze/blocklist/pause or seizure powers, upgradeability, and who controls those powers. Only admit currencies whose behavior is supported and whose issuer/governance risks are acceptable; reassess them when implementations or control change. Removing a currency prevents new listings and `updateListing` term changes but does not unwind existing escrow or eliminate exposure from existing sales. Existing bids, purchases, settlement, and payout-receiver repairs remain available subject to their other checks. Only standard ERC-20 tokens should be allowed. Rebasing, fee-on-transfer, and other tokens whose balances can change outside of transfers are not supported. Incoming transfers are checked for the full amount. Automatic payouts that report failure compare the auction house's balance: no movement creates a credit, an exact full-amount movement counts as paid, and any other movement reverts atomically rather than creating an unfunded credit. Escrow and payouts still assume balances held by the contract never change on their own (a negative rebase would leave escrowed bids and `claimableFunds` underfunded) and don't account for fees on outgoing transfers that report success. Low-decimal tokens should also be avoided so that bid increments stay meaningful.
- **ERC-20 issuer restrictions:** an allowlisted currency may still permit its issuer to freeze or block transfers. If outgoing transfers from the auction house are blocked after funds enter escrow, settlement can deliver the NFT while failed payouts become credits to their intended recipients, including the seller-selected payout receiver. Claiming to a different destination or retrying payment cannot bypass a restriction on the auction house as sender. Bid refunds and credited payouts remain subject to the token permitting outgoing transfers. The protocol intentionally retains pay-or-credit settlement rather than requiring immediate successful seller payment, so NFT delivery does not guarantee immediately transferable sale proceeds.
- **Settlement-time fees and royalties:** auction settlement intentionally uses the current protocol-fee settings, creator classification, and royalty configuration, including changes made after a bid is accepted. The protocol fee is capped at 5% and aggregate royalties at 10% of the gross sale value, bounding their combined deduction at 15%. These caps are the economic bounds; fees and royalty terms are not snapshotted at listing or first bid. Sellers accept that their net proceeds may change within those bounds while a bid prevents ordinary cancellation or listing updates. Previews are estimates based on current settings, not commitments to a fixed net payout; integrations should disclose these limits before listing.
- **Primary vs. secondary sales:** a sale is treated as primary (no royalties) when the seller matches the address returned by the `creatorLookup` helper. The default `CreatorLookup` returns the collection's current `owner`, which fits single-creator collections like Transient Labs creator contracts. For multi-creator collections, collections whose ownership has changed hands, or royalties paid to someone other than the collection owner (e.g. a collaborator split), a sale by the collection owner skips royalties. Classification is evaluated at settlement: transferring collection ownership to the seller can turn an already-funded secondary auction into a primary sale and suppress royalties, including those owed to independent beneficiaries. If the lookup is unset or fails, the sale is treated as secondary.
- **Royalty resolution:** for secondary sales, `RoyaltyLookup` uses the engine's schedule when it is correctly shaped and at least one of the first ten entries pays a nonzero amount to a nonzero recipient other than the calling auction house. Otherwise — a missing or codeless engine, a reverting call, mismatched arrays, or no payable entry within that recipient limit after the caller filter — it falls back to the NFT's direct ERC-2981 royalty. An intentional engine zero or a schedule payable only to the auction house does not suppress a nonzero direct royalty. The auction house calls the helper inside `try/catch` and applies its recipient filters and caps to the one schedule returned; schedules are never combined, and view and settlement use the same policy. Integrations should preview through `TLAuctionHouse.getRoyalty`; when directly calling `RoyaltyLookup.getRoyaltyView` with `eth_call`, they must set `from` to the auction-house address so its caller-dependent filtering matches settlement. If the NFT's `royaltyInfo` reverts, the helper returns no royalty. If it succeeds with malformed data (for example, a non-reverting fallback that returns nothing), decoding reverts the helper call, which the auction house catches, so the sale completes without a royalty. A reverting helper likewise results in no royalty. **Leaving the auction house's `royaltyLookup` unset (codeless) disables royalties.** Sufficient gas remains required.
- **Sanctions oracle dependency:** when configured, the oracle must successfully answer required screening queries. Oracle errors propagate and revert the transaction, including errors while screening payout recipients; failed screening does not create a fallback credit. Setting the oracle to `address(0)` explicitly disables screening. Delayed auction cancellation can still create credits and NFT claims without querying the oracle, but claims require successful screening while an oracle is configured.
- **Sanctions during auctions:** bids and top ups can still be placed after a seller becomes sanctioned, but settlement rechecks both the seller and winning bidder and cannot complete while either is sanctioned. If sanctions continue to block settlement, the winning bid can remain escrowed until the highest bidder's seven-day cancellation delay or the seller's 30-day cancellation delay passes. Cancellation credits the bidder's refund to `claimableFunds`; a sanctioned claimant cannot withdraw until no longer sanctioned. There is no immediate refund on a sanctions change.
- **Listing creation sequencing:** canceling a listing does not invalidate independently executable old `list` or `batchList` instructions in a seller's smart account or execution system. Sellers must sequence or invalidate those authorizations; if they remain executable, an old instruction may create a fresh listing at obsolete terms once the token is unlisted and back in that seller's custody. Current ownership and transfer approval remain required. No persistent creation-version parameter is enforced.
- **Seller update sequencing:** `updateListing` and `updatePayoutReceiver` act on the current listing without an expected listing ID. The caller must still be its seller. Sellers are responsible for sequencing or invalidating independently executable pending instructions and confirming the payout receiver, since an old authorized update can affect a replacement listing. Listing updates remain prohibited after bidding begins; payout updates remain available before settlement and do not change the listing ID.
- **Cancellation sequencing:** `delist` and `cancelUnsettledAuction` apply to the current listing and do not take an expected listing ID. Users and integrations must sequence or invalidate independently executable cancellation authorizations so an old instruction cannot unintentionally cancel a replacement listing. The caller must still be eligible on that current listing: delisting requires its seller and no bids; unsettled-auction cancellation requires its highest bidder after 7 days or its seller after 30 days. Ordinary sequential transactions from one EOA preserve that wallet's nonce order.
- **Unsettled auction cancellation:** the highest bidder can cancel after 7 days and the seller after 30 days even if settlement would succeed. Anyone can settle an ended auction before cancellation; the intended keeper service and bidder/seller reminders reduce the chance that a healthy auction reaches either cancellation window.
- **Trusted lookup adapters:** the protocol owner must select vetted creator and royalty lookup helpers that implement the expected interfaces and return correctly encoded data, and assess any proxy or upgrade authority over them. `RoyaltyLookup` uses its immediate caller to exclude an engine royalty payable to the consuming auction house, so it must be configured directly or behind a proxy that preserves `msg.sender`. A normal call-forwarding adapter is unsupported unless it independently applies the same caller-recipient filter before returning a schedule. A directly configured helper returning malformed successful data can cause auction-house decoding to revert despite `try/catch`, blocking settlement until the helper is corrected or replaced (or unset, which disables royalties). Delayed auction cancellation remains available. This is an accepted integration dependency. Listed NFT contracts are not trusted this way: the auction house never decodes an NFT's `royaltyInfo` itself, so a malformed NFT response reverts only the helper call and results in no royalty.
- **Automatic ETH payout gas:** `payoutGasLimit` starts at `1e5`, the same allowance as the previous `PAYOUT_GAS_LIMIT` constant; it is configurable so the allowance can follow future EVM gas-schedule changes. The protocol owner can change it to any nonzero value using `setPayoutGasLimit`, including while paused; `PayoutGasLimitUpdated(previousGasLimit, newGasLimit)` records changes. The live allowance applies separately to each automatic ETH refund, protocol fee, royalty, and seller payment. Failed calls credit the payee when enough transaction gas remains to finish. There is no hard upper bound: excessive settings can exhaust transaction gas, and up to ten royalty payments plus protocol and seller payments may each use the allowance. Owners should test settings and integrations should estimate the entire transaction. Explicit claims still forward available gas and revert atomically on failure; ERC-20 payouts and mint payments do not use this setting. Integrations reading the old `PAYOUT_GAS_LIMIT()` getter must use `payoutGasLimit()` for this version.
- **Creator and royalty lookup gas:** these external lookups intentionally have no explicit gas caps, preserving compatibility with integrations whose gas requirements vary. `try/catch` does not guarantee enough gas remains to finish settlement after a lookup consumes its forwarded gas. A hostile or unusually expensive NFT/lookup can therefore prevent settlement. After the auction ends, the highest bidder can cancel after 7 days and the seller after 30 days without calling the NFT or lookup, crediting the bidder and creating an NFT claim for the seller. Claiming the NFT still requires a successful transfer.
- **TRACE physical COAs:** TRACE is a physical certificate-of-authenticity product and is not intended to be auctioned through Stacks. Its collection owner/admin retains privileged transfer authority, including the ability to move a token out of auction escrow. This intended-use limitation is not enforced by an on-chain listing restriction. If custody is lost and delivery fails, settlement reverts; delayed cancellation can credit the bidder, but a seller NFT claim cannot recover a token no longer held by Stacks.
- **NFT transfer policies:** a collection's transfer validator may allow escrow deposits but reject delivery, return to the seller, or a particular recipient. Collection authorities may also change validator policy after listing or bidding; in supported TL creator contracts, a change from zero to positive royalties can activate an already-configured validator. Successful escrow ingress does not guarantee that later transfers will succeed. If delivery fails, settlement reverts before releasing sale proceeds. After the auction ends, the highest bidder can cancel after 7 days and the seller after 30 days to credit the bidder without calling the NFT contract. The seller's resulting NFT claim still requires a permitted transfer. Choosing another claim destination may help with recipient-specific restrictions, but Stacks cannot override a policy that blocks transfers out of escrow; collection-authority intervention may be required, and NFT recovery is not guaranteed. Sellers and bidders accept these external transfer-policy dependencies and the potential recovery delay.
- **Marketplace transfer-policy checks:** integrating marketplaces such as Transient should consider checking supported collection transfer policies before offering listings. Where the validator exposes reliable checks, assess escrow ingress, return to the seller, and delivery to a known recipient; inspect configured validators even when zero royalties currently bypass validation. Flag or prevent listings with known incompatible policies. An auction winner may not be known at listing time, so recipient-specific restrictions should also be checked when bidding where supported. These checks describe current compatibility, not guaranteed future transferability: collection policy can change, arbitrary NFT behavior may not be predictable, and users can interact directly with the contracts.

## Deployments
Contracts are deployed to the same address cross-chain using CREATE2. In doing such, constructor args have to be set to default values and then changed after deployment. Contracts are initially deployed with an EOA as the owner until initializations are made, then subsequently contract ownership is transferred to multi-sigs. Ownership transfers are two-step (`Ownable2Step`), so the multi-sig must call `acceptOwnership` to complete the transfer.

### Protocol administration and recipient checks
The three sales contracts (`TLAuctionHouse`, `TLStacks721`, and `TLStacks1155`) disable ownership renunciation. Ownership can still be transferred through the existing two-step process; naming the zero address as pending owner cancels a pending transfer. Collection ownership and admin handovers remain the collection team's responsibility.

Each sales contract rejects its own address as an explicitly configured fee receiver, seller payout receiver, or NFT recipient. The auction house also rejects itself as a funds/NFT claim destination. Other contract recipients remain supported subject to their transfer behavior; callers must choose a usable destination. Auction royalties naming the auction house continue to be skipped.

Pausing does not stop auction clocks or extend deadlines. Auctions can expire while paused; the incumbent may win without further bidding once settlement resumes. Protocol owners should account for this when pausing.

### Audit integration updates

Protocol-owner fee calls now require the identity observed when preparing the instruction: `waiveSellerFee(nftAddress, expectedRound)` for ERC-721, `waiveSellerFee(nftAddress, tokenId, expectedRound)` for ERC-1155, and `removeProtocolFee(nftAddress, tokenId, expectedListingId)` for auctions. Read that identity before submitting; passing a newly fetched identity automatically when retrying would waive the new sale instead. Existing applied auction waivers survive `updateListing`; pending waivers against the old ID revert. The unchecked signatures were removed.

A verified new NFT listing deposit atomically retires any previous recovery claim for that token and emits `NftClaimRetired`. If transfer verification or any item in a batch fails, the previous claim is preserved. Token-reported ownership is authoritative: this prevents an old claimant withdrawing a successor listing's escrow, but cannot detect privileged transfers out and back between protocol calls. Bidder funds credits remain intact.

`ListingConfigured`, `AuctionBid`, and `AuctionBidToppedUp` emit the stored listing after external callbacks, so an authorized callback update cannot leave the final event stale. Terminal settlement/cancellation events retain their historical listing payloads. No existing event signatures or drop round fields were changed.
